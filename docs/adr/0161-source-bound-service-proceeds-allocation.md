# 0161 — Source-bound service proceeds allocation

The service financial flow allocates original proceeds into owner, company, provider and tax entitlement. Cash collection establishes custody and unallocated ownership first; allocation cannot create another receipt or change the physical location of funds. Completed service evidence, native collection identity and the original policy snapshot must bind each admission. Later service orders must reference this original financial source rather than fabricate a second one.

Allocation rules belong in versioned configuration. Each explicit recipe states its service, currency, fixed obligations, proportions of proceeds remaining after those obligations, remainder beneficiary and rounding priority. Fixed obligations cannot exceed actual proceeds. Apportion the remaining minor units by largest fractional remainder, with the configured priority resolving exact ties. Preserve every cent and validate PostgreSQL monetary limits without JavaScript monetary floats. A company portion represents its configured entitlement before any separately recognized costs; an estimated internal cost cannot create a duplicate provider payable.

Tax assessment remains a separate policy interface. Every recipe identifies the tax decisions it requires, and every allocation must match an independent assessment for each decision, including an explicitly approved zero assessment. Allocation chooses no legal rate, exemption or accounting treatment. Missing policy or assessment denies admission.

Service assessment reuses the original `finance.tax` fields and immutable policy version. Explicit assessment metadata selects the actual collected-gross basis, fixed amount or basis-point method and the native Money rounding convention. Missing metadata, ambiguous keys, currency mismatch or unsupported sub-minor-unit results deny admission. Service allocation and tax configuration use the same source-authority implementation as the existing financial recurrence controls, including final revocation fences and private audit exports.

Complete allocation history requires an unconditional service-record grant at its actual scope, plus independent complete financial and party source access. Property scopes remain meaningful. A cap on a small original collection cannot authorize disclosure of larger historical provider costs. The original organization-source check retains its exact semantics through the shared unconditional-source helper; held and current authority are both required.

Admission snapshots retain only the selected service recipe, tax assessments and their immutable rule identities. They exclude unrelated services and tax catalog entries. Provider bindings are append-only, versioned associations to original recognized supplier lines or employee costs. Releases preserve prior admissions. Each admission, complete provider reference set and source audit commit atomically, and a repeated command recovers its original result. Financial inspection uses canonical task evidence internally without requiring or granting access to operational task views.

The general revenue journal combines company and provider portions into one company-revenue credit. Individual provider amounts and identities remain in the independently protected admission and native payable sources; general journal lines cannot reveal compensation by splitting that credit.

Provider entitlements must preserve their actual supplier/person identity and join the canonical payable source and payment history. Owner portions must retain the original property ownership evidence. Financial commands must intersect held and current source authority, use native journal, period and lock helpers, commit source acknowledgement and audit atomically, and correct history through exact opposite entries. Estimates, incomplete service records and unverified tax assessments cannot authorize postings.

Provider accounting treatment is an explicit configuration decision, with no default. Under the supported `REVENUE_WITH_EXISTING_COMPANY_COST` recipe, an original company-funded invoice or employee cost already recognized the provider obligation, and its native expense/payable journal remains authoritative. Service allocation credits company service revenue for both the company portion and that already recognized provider cost, while retaining the actual provider source identity. The original expense reduces company net position; allocation creates no second expense or payable. A 500 MAD collection with 100 owner entitlement, 300 recognized provider cost and 100 company portion therefore leaves location 500, owner payable 100, provider payable 300 and company net 100. Whole-invoice and employee-claim payments keep their native scope. Missing or different accounting treatment is denied; other approved treatments require their own policy adapter.

Closed source periods use the existing accounting-period adjustment record. The tenant-scoped service-posting reference is additive; the original expense, invoice and employee source branches remain unchanged. First recognition retains the actual service completion date unless the operator explicitly requests a current-open-period adjustment and holds independent service and journal override authority at both dates. Database guards bind the new recognition, original admission, latest closed-period decision and complete private audits in the same transaction. Exact amounts, owners, taxes and journal dimensions stay fixed. Corrections append the native exact opposite in the current open period, including when the original month has closed, without changing its frozen snapshot.

The private workspace reuses native input schemas, source inspectors, field presentation, current-authority fences and command hooks. Only a scoped random nonce may survive a reload. Recovery shares the native command lock, requires the original actor and repeats complete current source access before returning its acknowledgement; absence is never reported while the original command can still commit. Original admissions and journal acknowledgements supply receipts directly, so the workspace adds no second command or payable ledger.

`OWNER_SERVICES` predates the allocation producer and remains a reporting bucket for direct ownership history. A bucket alone cannot identify a new service allocation. The journal fence instead protects explicit service identities, reserved service revenue/tax accounts, and movements between unallocated collections and owner payables, including attempts to remove the original labels or markers. This preserves existing direct bank-funded owner history while retaining mandatory native acknowledgements for service movements. All applied SQL is preserved; the correction is an additive migration.

Provider payment history delegates to the original supplier-invoice or employee-claim readers. Payments and outstanding amounts retain that whole-payable scope, rather than inventing a payment allocation to the associated service cost. Native payment opposites, source reversals, original identities and independent current salary access remain visible or protected through those original readers and the final service-source disclosure fence.
